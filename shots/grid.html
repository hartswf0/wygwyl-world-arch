The Grid

He stands on an empty floor. Every word he says sets down one real thing cut out of the shots for that scene, at the distance it was measured from the camera. It arrives pale, like an unwashed print. When the line ends a wave goes out from him and washes it blue.

How it works

One word, one thing

The line he is saying belongs to a scene; the scene has shots; the shots have named, masked objects. Each word takes the next object, far things first. If the word is the object's own name, that object jumps the queue and keeps its label.

Where it lands

At its measured bearing and distance from that shot's camera, with him standing where the camera stood. Far distances are compressed so the whole scene fits on the floor; each thing keeps the size it had in the picture.

Exposure, then water

A thing arrives as a pale latent print. The wave at the end of the line develops it, as water does a cyanotype. COLOUR shows the cut-outs as they were shot.

Around the clock

Each scene of the poem takes its own bearing around him, in order. By the end he is standing inside a ring of everything he said.

Inverse topology — the shots shape the land

We already have extraordinary shots for these poems: the generated stills of every shot in the bible, the MARKOV_POET clips routed stanza by stanza, the archive footage the Cineosis forage found for each beat, the Halfworld frames. Forward, a world is grown and then photographed. Here it runs the other way: start from the best images for each poem and ask what the land must be for that frame to exist — then sculpt every world toward it.

THE SHOTS
CHOOSECLIP ranks every image against its poem’s place, light and set pieces — and against every other poem
READa panoptic segmentation into the world’s own classes — sky, water, city, street, forest, meadow, beach, rock — plus horizon
FINDsearch each world for the camera that already comes closest: position on the poem’s ground, heading, height
INFERevery missing region becomes a place: its column a bearing, its row below the horizon a distance, its height an elevation
SCULPTcarve the water, lay the beach, plant the forest, raise the ridge to its angle, stand the city up to its skyline
BUILD THROUGHcut panoptic apertures & stage our multiplane cut-out cast directly inside the sculpted terrain

Bigger than this — now built & interactive

Clips, not stills. A clip is a camera path and a duration. Read its motion (pan, dolly, rise) and the inverse gives a route through the land, not a point.
The reference as a reader. Beside the poems and the builder, a third reader: can this world give back its shots? Evolve against it, and worlds are bred by their photographs.
Cross-poem finds are places. When CLIP pulls the same chapel image for poem 3 and poem 11, the images are saying what the bible says: one site at two times.
Interiors become sets. References with no horizon — the bedroom, the party, the hall — shape walk-in sets: their walls, light, windows looking out onto the Foundry world, and cut-out cast.
Every shot a triple. The found image, the staged cut-out set, the rendered world: the film cuts and blends between all three, each matched to the same line.

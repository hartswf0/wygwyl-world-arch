# rigkit: layered painted rigs

rigkit makes a character move from his own paintings. The method is H3 from SCION 2:
- **Rig:** one painting is cut into overlapping layers. Each layer is a soft-skinned mesh,
  so joints bend the painted cloth instead of hinging cut cards.
- **Parts:** heads, hands and costume details are drawings cut from all of that
  character's paintings, swapped in per pose.

The pipeline poses the rig automatically from the Pose Lab markers and animates pose to
pose to a poem's word timings.

Run everything from `shots/scion2/`, so the package resolves as `rigkit`. You need
python3 with numpy, scipy and OpenCV, plus ffmpeg.

## Pipeline

| step | call | writes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. rigs | `python3 -m rigkit.core <char> [view...]`, or `core.build_rig(char, view, master_painting=None)` | `rigkit/build/<char>/rig/<view>/` (`layer_*.png`, `edge_*.png`, `brad.png`, `master.png`, `rig.json`) |
| 2. parts | `python3 -m rigkit.parts <char>`, or `parts.build_parts(char)` | `rigkit/build/<char>/parts/` (`<painting>--head.png`, `--handR/L.png`, curated parts, `index.json`) |
| 3. pose | `posing.pose_from_markers(char, painting_id, keys=None)` | returns a pose dict |
| 4. render | `posing.render(pose) -> RGBA` (on the painting's canvas) | |
| 5. all poses | `python3 -m rigkit.cast <char>` | `shots/scion2/cast/<char>/poses/*.png`, `contact_N.jpg`, `poses.json`, `flags.json` |
| 6. animate | `animate.animate(char, keys, out_mp4, duration, audio=..., strip=...)` | mp4 (+ audio), strip jpg |
| 7. a poem | `python3 -m rigkit.poem shots/scion2/poems/<NN>/plan.json` | `poem<NN>.mp4`, `poem<NN>_strip.jpg`, `poem<NN>.json` |

`build/` is regenerated by steps 1 and 2 and is not committed. It takes about 30 s.

## A character module: `chars/<char>__<costume>.py`
This is the only per-character code. `chars/poet__red_black_sunglasses.py` is the worked
example. A module defines the following:

| name | what |
|---|---|
| `MASTERS = {view: (painting id, builder)}` | One master painting per rig. The builder is either a function `fn(rgb, alpha) -> spec` with hand-drawn layer cuts (the poet's `front` and `q3r`) or the string `'auto'`. `'auto'` cuts the layers automatically from the painting's markers (`auto.py`); the poet's `kneel`, `sit`, `lie` and `crouch3q` rigs use it. Start a new character with `'auto'` everywhere and hand-cut only the rigs that show defects. |
| `MASTER_FACING = {view: 'R' or 'L' or None}` | Which way each master faces. A target facing the other way is rendered mirrored. |
| `VIEW_RIGS = {catalog view: (rig, mirrored)}`, `POSTURE_RIGS = {posture: rig}` | How a painting's view or posture picks a rig. |
| `BRAD_SOURCE = (painting, cx, cy, brass r, sprite r)` | The one clean brad drawn at every joint. Omit it, and the `brads` lists, for a character without brads. |
| `DEPTH` | Draw order of bones inside a layer: distal over proximal. |
| `catalog()` | Optional per-painting hints (`view`, `cls` posture, `costume`, `use`, `gaze`, ...). For the poet these come from H2's hand reading (`../h2/tools/catalog_src.py`). Without a catalog, the view is guessed from the markers: shoulder width against torso length, and which side the chin is on. |
| `is_character_painting(id, cat)`, `painting_flags(id, cat)` | Which paintings may give parts, and why a target is suspect (another character, a fragment, a crop). |
| `CURATED_PARTS` | Parts that need a hand-made cut, as a polygon or circle on a source painting with its wrist or anchor point: fists from a sheet without markers, a phone in the hand, the poet.04 chest emblem. |
| `COSTUME_PROPS`, `PROP_SPINE_FRAC` | Costume details added per costume, such as the emblem on the poet.04 suit, and where they sit on the spine. |
| `KEYS = {painting id: overrides}` | Per-pose fixes. The available keys are listed below. |

### Per-pose override keys
`view` (`'front'`, `'q3r'`, `'q3r_m'` for mirrored, ...), `head` (`[part id, mirrored]`),
`hands` (`{'R': [part id, mirrored], 'L': ...}`), `wristR`/`wristL` and `head_tilt`
(degrees), `bones` (absolute bone angles in degrees), `add` (degrees added), `set`,
`len`, `scale`, `offset`, `props`, `no_costume_props`.

## How each stage works
- **Layers.** Each rig has a legs layer, a torso layer, a head layer and one layer per
  arm. The far arm in a 3/4 view sits behind the torso.
  - **Hidden areas:** the jacket under the arms, the trousers under the hem and the neck
    under the jaw are filled in from the painting's own plain fabric.
  - **Jacket:** it is a rounded body shape, so raised arms leave no square sleeve-cap
    tabs.
- **Mesh.** Each layer is a 2 px triangle grid over its alpha. The vertices are weighted
  to bone segments by a softmax of distance. The torso blends chest to pelvis along the
  spine, and its shoulder caps follow the upper arms.
- **Rendering.** The mesh is rasterized into a per-pixel lookup into the master texture
  (3x upsampled), so every pixel is a painting pixel, moved. Each pass runs from the
  master and never from a previous frame. Each piece then gets one crisp paper edge at
  output resolution (dark brown line plus tan hairline). Seams meant to be continuous
  cloth get none.
- **Brads.** Every painted brad is removed from the layers by inpainting. One clean
  source brad (`BRAD_SOURCE`) is then drawn once, crisp and unrotated, at each joint.
- **Parts.** Every painting with markers gives its own head and its own two hands.
  - **Hands:** skin pixels beyond the wrist marker.
  - **Head:** a head-shaped window above the collar, narrowing at the neck so no shoulder
    or collar is taken.
  - **Seat:** each part records where it was cut (neck point and torso angle, or wrist
    point and forearm angle) and the source torso length. It is placed on the rig at the
    same anatomical point, rotated by the bone's change in angle and scaled by torso
    length.
  - **Quality check:** a part fails (`ok: false`) when it is cut by the sheet's straight
    edge or has an implausible area.
- **Posing.** Bone angles are the rotation from the master's marker-to-marker direction
  to the target's. Squash along the bone, clamped, gives foreshortening. Beyond 35° of
  spine lean, the whole figure turns. The automatic choices are:
  - **rig:** posture rig, then view rig, then mirroring;
  - **head and hands:** the target painting's own head and hands if they cut cleanly;
    otherwise the head whose catalog view and gaze match best, and the rig's painted
    hand.
- **Animation (`animate.py`).**
  - **Rig changes:** each move between two keys is rendered in the rig of the next key.
    On a held frame, the figure is first re-solved in that rig to the previous pose. So a
    rig or view change lands on a held frame, never mid-motion.
  - **Easing:** 3 frames of anticipation, then a back-out ease (overshoot and settle).
  - **Feet:** between two standing keys in the same rig, the feet are planted by
    two-bone IK. A foot that must travel steps on an arc while the other stays planted.
  - **Swaps:** hands swap on the landing frame. The head swaps 3 frames later when both
    change.
  - **Holds:** they breathe.
- **Poems (`poem.py`).** Each key lands on the start of a named word, chosen near a given
  time from `shots/stage/timeline/<NN>.json`. The audio is muxed. By default one costume
  is kept through a poem, so costume props stay off.

## Adding a new character
1. Make sure it has markers in `shots/rig/poses/<char>__<costume>.json` (or `poses_v2`).
2. Copy the poet module. Set `MASTERS` with `'auto'` builders on a few clean paintings:
   one front, one 3/4, and any posture rigs it needs. Set `MASTER_FACING`, and either
   drop or replace the brad and emblem settings.
3. Run the rig, parts and cast steps. Look at `contact_*.jpg` and `flags.json`, then fix
   rigs by hand-cutting the layers (see `build_front`) and poses with `KEYS`.

## Known limits
See the report in `shots/scion2/cast/poet__red_black_sunglasses/flags.json` and the
poem's `poem09.json`.
- **Turns are swaps.** A front to 3/4 change is a rig swap (a cut on a held frame), not a
  turn.
- **Auto-cut rigs are rougher.** The kneel, sit, lie and crouch rigs show filled-in
  waists, soft seat areas and occasional prop scraps from their master, such as the
  letter in the crouch master's hand.
- **Heads keep their painted size.** A replacement head is scaled by torso length, so a
  painting drawn with a bigger head keeps it.
- **No head turn inside a pose.** A head that turns relative to the rig is a different
  drawing.

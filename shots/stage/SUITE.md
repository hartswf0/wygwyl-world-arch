# The cut-out suite: the stage as a player

`shots/stage/index.html?p=NN` plays each poem scene by scene on her recorded reading. The stage cuts when her voice reaches the next scene. Inside a scene, layers show, hide, travel, swap pose and start their solved actions on her words. Everything the stage did before still works: scene strip, layers, the tray, direct manipulation, blend and opacity, per-scene saves, and PNG/JSON export.

```
python3 shots/stage/build_timeline.py          # all 14 timelines (or: ... 04 07)
open shots/stage/index.html?p=07               # Space plays; ?t=33.4 starts there; ?play=1 autoplays
```

## The clock

Her voice is the master clock. This is the weave's rule (`weave.html`: `t = audio.currentTime` while the audio plays, the wall clock otherwise) on the suite player's single shared timeline (`wygwyl-suite-player.html`: one audio element, and the film is chosen by where `currentTime` falls).

- Each animation frame reads `t` from the audio element and quantizes it to a 24 fps frame (`f = floor(t·24)`). The stage only redraws when `f` changes.
- The audio is never seeked per frame. This follows `butterfly-halfworld/wygwyl/suite.html`, where setting `currentTime` restarts the decoder and stutters. The element runs free and is moved only when the user seeks.
- The mp3 is fetched whole and played from a blob URL, so seeking never waits on HTTP range requests. The plain `http.server` does not serve those.
- If the audio is blocked or missing, the same code runs on the wall clock and says so.
- **Scene cuts.** The scene shown is the one whose `[t0, t1)` holds `t`. Scenes are not assumed to follow the book order. In poems 11 and 13 her reading takes two scenes in a different order from the scene book, and the stage follows her voice.
- **Action layers.** Solved Arena clips are drawn at a frame computed from the same `t`:
  - after a `play` cue: `floor((t − t_cue)·24)`. With `mode: once` it holds on the last frame; with `mode: loop` it wraps.
  - with no cue: `floor(t·24)`, looped.

  So a scrub, a pause or a seek shows exactly the frame the playback would show.

## Cues

A cue is anchored to a word, not to a time:

```json
{"w": 512, "word": "walked", "t": 33.41, "layer": "action:ACT-001__man", "action": "play", "mode": "once"}
```

On load `t = words[w].s + (dt || 0)`, so the cue moves with the word if the timings are ever redone. `layer` is a ref (the part id, `action:<id>` or the image src, plus `#n` for the n-th duplicate), not an index. Reordering layers does not break a cue.

| action | effect |
| :-- | :-- |
| `show` / `hide` | Toggle the layer. With `fx: "wipe"`, the archive wipes in from the top over 0.5 s, or back up on hide. With `fx: "pop"`, it fades in over 0.25 s. |
| `move` | `dx, dy` px over `dur` s, eased. Moves add up. |
| `play` | Shows an action layer and starts its clip at this word (`mode: once \| loop`). |
| `pose-swap` | Puts another drawn pose of the poem (`part`) in this layer's place. It keeps the sheet's drawing scale and keeps the feet where they were. |

The state of every layer is a pure function of `t`: the base layer, then every cue with `cue.t ≤ t` in order. Scrubbing backwards is therefore exact. This is the suite's `renderField(local)` idea: the picture is computed from `t`, not accumulated.

**The cue log.** While playing, cues *crossed* between the previous frame and this one are logged under the timeline, so a seek does not replay them. This is how the suite fires foley. It is the stage's version of the weave's contact log: every join that fired, with its word.

**Plans change underneath.** Plans are regenerated by `plan_poems.py`. When a cue's exact layer is gone, it falls back to the layer in the same role: the poet's drawn pose, the scene's action, or the k-th tagged archive still. The cue is then marked as a stand-in. A cue with no stand-in is struck through in the lane and the list. Re-run `build_timeline.py` after the plans are regenerated.

## Auto cues (first pass, `auto: true`)

- **play**: the scene's hidden solved action starts on its verb (`ACT_VERBS`: walk, fall, turn, dance, wait, kneel …), and the still pose hides on the same word. If no verb is said, it starts on the first content word of the second line (`why: "fallback: second line"`, 40 of 52 action cues).
- **pose-swap**: another of the poem's main poses comes in on a word that names it. For example, "heart" in 04.2 swaps *kneel hollow* for *heart seated*, and "harvest" in 13.4 brings in *harvest reach*.
- **move**: the figure travels on walk / follow / run / ride words, over the rest of the line.
- **props**: a prop whose name she says is held back from the scene start and pops in on that word. Two at most per scene.
- **archive**: the scene's first hidden, tagged still wipes in on a word of the scene's name (or mid-scene) and wipes out at the end of that line. Its tag shows for exactly as long as any of it shows, and overlapping tags stack instead of printing over each other.

All 14 poems: 276 auto cues.

## Editing

1. Select a layer on the stage or in the layer list.
2. Choose a cue type: show, show · wipe in, hide, hide · wipe out, play once, play loop, move →, move ←, or pose-swap (pick the pose).
3. Click a word in the lane under the bar. The word anchors the cue.

Clicking a word with nothing selected only seeks. Cues are listed per scene. Click one to go to it, or ✕ to remove it. Reset cues returns the scene to the timeline file.

Edits save per scene in `localStorage` (`wygwyl.stage.NN.KEY.cues`), alongside the layer saves (`wygwyl.stage.NN.KEY`). **Export poem JSON** writes each scene with its `t0`, `t1` and `cues`, plus `timeline`, `audio` and `fps`.

**Keys:**
- Space: play / pause
- `,` / `.`: one frame back / forward (paused)
- Drag the bar to scrub.
- Clicking a scene in the strip seeks to its `t0`.

## Alignment

The scene book lines (`poems/atlas/NN.json`) are an abridged paraphrase of her reading. The word timings (`wygwyl/timings/NN.json`, Whisper small.en, 94–293 words per poem) are what she actually said.

1. **Place each scene.** Each scene's book tokens are aligned inside her words by a dynamic-programming alignment:
   - fuzzy word match: exact, shared stem, or edit ratio ≥ 0.75
   - a book word that is not found costs 0.45
   - a reading word that is skipped costs 0.06
   - free lead-in and tail

   Function words count as anchors only within two words of a content-word match. Without that rule, a stray "the" pulls a scene far off.
2. **Resolve order and repeats.** Scenes are placed strongest first, and never over a span another scene already holds. This handles repeated passages (06 reads "Here we are all one … all safe" twice; 04 says "What if I follow this …" twice) and lines read out of book order (11, 13).
3. **Scenes with no anchors.** Such a scene takes its book-order slot and a share of the gap proportional to its length.
4. **Cuts.** Each cut sits on her last breath before the scene's first anchored word: the latest pause of 0.3 s or more, or a new Whisper phrase, between the previous scene's last anchor and that word. The cut lands half the pause (at most 0.3 s) before the word, snapped to a 24 fps frame. Every scene lasts at least 1 s.

`match` in each scene records how it was placed, its coverage (the share of book tokens found), its span and its first word.

## What was taken from where

- **The weave** (`shots/weave.html`, `weave/README.md`, `clips/p04_hands.json`, `p07_turn_me_up.json`)
  - States and beats are timed to her words (`words: [{w, t0, t1}]`, states on word times).
  - The audio is the clock, with a wall-clock fallback.
  - Archive is labelled for exactly as long as it shows.
  - The **wipe** join: archive registered and wiped in on a line, back out on another.
  - Contacts are logged, which here becomes the cue log.
- **butterfly-halfworld `suite.html` + `worlds/NN-*.mjs`**
  - Every mechanism is a line: here every cue is a word, with a `why`.
  - A cue fires on *crossing* its beat, not on being past it.
  - The record runs free and is only corrected, never seeked per frame.
  - A strip of films with movements inside: here the bar of scenes with words inside.
- **prompt-language `wygwyl-suite-player.html`**
  - One audio element is the clock for many films. The current film comes from `currentTime` by search. The poem's lines are shown from the beat the clock is in. The scrub timeline marks each film's window.
  - Here that becomes the bar of scene windows with word ticks and cue marks, the caption lit word by word, and the clock and frame readout.

## Verified

Headless Chromium (puppeteer, `--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required`, `python3 -m http.server 8774`) on poems 01, 04, 05, 07, 11, 13 and 14:

- no console errors and no failed requests;
- seeks inside four scenes per poem show the right scene, caption, played actions and swapped poses;
- playing across a scene boundary runs on the audio clock (`wall: false`), cuts to the expected scene and logs the crossed cues;
- selecting a layer and clicking a word adds a cue and saves it to `localStorage`.

## Limits

- The cues are a first pass. In 40 of the 52 scenes with a solved action, the action starts on the fallback word because her line has no verb for it.
- The pose-swap and prop matches are keyword matches on card names, so they find "heart", "harvest" and "hallway", not meanings.
- The word timings are Whisper's, so word edges are good to about ±0.1 s.
- Scene windows depend on the scene book. Where the book paraphrases heavily (04.3, 06.2, 06.4), the window is approximate, and the strip shows `~` for an interpolated scene.

POEM 12 · The White Temple & its Pools

Reunion

Decades later, the poet hosts his brothers and friends in the white temple he built in 11: hugs with no reason, laughter, football on a wall screen, an old snapshot restaged. Underneath the noise one quantity only grows, the distance between two brothers, from a hug to talking distance to the width of a memory, while a yard of burning hourglasses goes out one by one until a single one is left, and its last ember drifts out over the sea through the gap the wind opened.

Bone-white plaster #ece6da and warm sandstone #c99765 (the poem's own colour in film/poems.json) come from the colonnaded pavilion in 08fe12af_0; pool turquoise #2fa3a8 is the same still's water; #8a6a4a is the dark wood of the hourglass ends and the low benches. Dusk indigo #3a3f63 and near-black #14121c are the night mirror corridor in 58cbb4ca_0, used for the ELDERS movement when the polarity turns over; the violet star-fleck #7b6fd6 is the starlit black pool of f05c1fa5_1 (the afterlives). The one hot accent is ember orange #e0632a: hourglass flames and the last drifting ember, nothing else in the poem is allowed to be that colour.

The White Temple is the Shore realm's one interior: storm and ruin outside, a hall full of people inside, decades later. Paint it as the city's purest ceramic: bone-white plates on the chapel's old dark stone, every plate joint drawn with a thin cyan seam (the brightest seam in the city; a Glazier drew it), and the chapel's flood line kept as a faint grey-blue tide mark at 4/10 of wall height under the vault, never painted over. Light is not lamps but the white ceramic itself lit by the sky: code one warm bounce gradient on the white wall cards that cools from 17:30 gold to 19:45 blue, and let the only point lights be the hourglass flames, the wall screen, and at the end a single porch bulb. Floors are mirror pools: a black gloss card under each set that doubles whatever stands on it (code a flipped, darkened, 2px-rippled copy). The hall is wide and symmetrical, shot down the nave; outside, the camera goes low to the water. Never show the city's neon here, never a second light colour besides warm amber and seam cyan, and never a clean wall at the flood line.

Hero buildings

The Temple's Pools

The hall's floor and the terrace are both pools: every hug in 12.0 is doubled in the black floor, and the last ember (and then the porch bulb) is reflected in the pool of stars. The portico pool gives the 11 to 12 cut: same frame, filled with people.

Build as cards: Three gloss-black floor cards (hall floor, portico pool, star pool) with a coded reflection pass: flipped copy of the layers above, 60% darkness, 2px horizontal ripple that settles. The portico pool is a facade card of seven white columns with two tree cards either side; the star pool has a mitred stone rim card in the foreground and a star field painted on the water card that stays put while the sky drifts. Tambourine fragments from 03 are set into the pool rim as small white plates.

Insights

  • The poem is about distance that grows while love does not shrink: keep the brothers' gap a single number that only widens (hug ~15, talking 28-52, memory 54-70, terrace wider) and never animate them walking toward each other.
  • Words shrink, they never erode: the printed word cards go smaller movement to movement; a half-dissolved glyph is noise.
  • ELDERS is the one polarity flip in the film: paper becomes ink and the elders are holes in it; build them as negative cut-outs in a drop, not as puppets, and let the drop drain rather than the elders walk off.
  • Twelve hourglasses on a grid, nudged clear of the brothers' heads (flames off a crown read as a candelabra), going out in a coprime order; the count of the ones still lit IS the question 'is this a one-time occasion?'.
  • Orange is reserved for fire; the temple is bone, sandstone and turquoise by day and indigo/violet by night.
  • Transcript corrections from the treatment: 'our glasses' is 'hourglasses'; 'shared years' is 'shared airs' in the treatment, check against the voice before setting type.
  • Atlas: the foundry's ink hourglass drawing (foundry/12-03) is the right graphic language for the whole hourglass yard: flat black wood ends, dotted sand, notched flame cards. Do not render glass realistically.
  • Atlas: the reunion is shot down the nave in symmetry and doubled in a black floor; one gloss reflection pass on the floor card does more for 'the white temple' than any wall detail.
  • Atlas: keep the chapel's flood line visible under the white plates; it is what makes this the same building as 03 and 11.
  • Atlas: the archive's group portraits (archive/2696, 6946) fix the snapshot composition: frontal rows, seated in front, everyone looking at camera; the restaging must match it card for card.

For later poems

  • temple_hall and the terrace become the harbor edge of 13 and the 'temple' that turns to haze in 14.
  • The burning hourglass is the ninth and last motif card in 14's reel.
  • The family kit (brother, friends, elders) is reused for 13's couples and elders and 14's everyday people.
  • Sunglasses come off on the terrace; in 13 taking them off becomes the refocus.

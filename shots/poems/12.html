POEM 12 · The White Temple & its Pools

Reunion

Decades later, the poet hosts his brothers and friends in the white temple he built in 11: hugs with no reason, laughter, football on a wall screen, an old snapshot restaged. Underneath the noise one quantity only grows, the distance between two brothers, from a hug to talking distance to the width of a memory, while a yard of burning hourglasses goes out one by one until a single one is left, and its last ember drifts out over the sea through the gap the wind opened.

Bone-white plaster #ece6da and warm sandstone #c99765 (the poem's own colour in film/poems.json) come from the colonnaded pavilion in 08fe12af_0; pool turquoise #2fa3a8 is the same still's water; #8a6a4a is the dark wood of the hourglass ends and the low benches. Dusk indigo #3a3f63 and near-black #14121c are the night mirror corridor in 58cbb4ca_0, used for the ELDERS movement when the polarity turns over; the violet star-fleck #7b6fd6 is the starlit black pool of f05c1fa5_1 (the afterlives). The one hot accent is ember orange #e0632a: hourglass flames and the last drifting ember, nothing else in the poem is allowed to be that colour.

Insights

  • The poem is about distance that grows while love does not shrink: keep the brothers' gap a single number that only widens (hug ~15, talking 28-52, memory 54-70, terrace wider) and never animate them walking toward each other.
  • Words shrink, they never erode: the printed word cards go smaller movement to movement; a half-dissolved glyph is noise.
  • ELDERS is the one polarity flip in the film: paper becomes ink and the elders are holes in it; build them as negative cut-outs in a drop, not as puppets, and let the drop drain rather than the elders walk off.
  • Twelve hourglasses on a grid, nudged clear of the brothers' heads (flames off a crown read as a candelabra), going out in a coprime order; the count of the ones still lit IS the question 'is this a one-time occasion?'.
  • Orange is reserved for fire; the temple is bone, sandstone and turquoise by day and indigo/violet by night.
  • Transcript corrections from the treatment: 'our glasses' is 'hourglasses'; 'shared years' is 'shared airs' in the treatment, check against the voice before setting type.

For later poems

  • temple_hall and the terrace become the harbor edge of 13 and the 'temple' that turns to haze in 14.
  • The burning hourglass is the ninth and last motif card in 14's reel.
  • The family kit (brother, friends, elders) is reused for 13's couples and elders and 14's everyday people.
  • Sunglasses come off on the terrace; in 13 taking them off becomes the refocus.

POEM 09 · The Shrouded Arcade

Yet, Heard

Three calls made at the edge of a harbour before leaving: to the lover under a shared moon, to the mother over a black hole that opens in the water, to the father who walks out of the eastern sky. One night on one quay, and the light is the clock: moon high, the dead hour, first grey, the sun coming up out of the harbour mouth. Each call is a dashed line of light strung between two people, and behind every call the same breakup keeps playing on the sidewalk.

Night navy and harbour teal from the bay stills (3238a164 lamps on black water, ef9f6c7e night quay); sodium amber of the quay lamps and the town across the water from the same two; neon red only in the club sidewalk opening (b05d5b9a puddle, 7fdacba9 street); the moon is paper cream, never pure white. Dawn walks the sky from slate grey (#8a8f9a) to sunrise apricot (#f2b35a) in M3-M4. Electric blue (#2f6bff) is the poem's single accent: 'someone blue', one point of light on the poet's chest in the last few seconds, nowhere else.

From our footage

The strongest elements in what we already have, and what each is for.

light

lamps reflected as broken vertical streaks on black water under a quay wall and bridge piers; teal and sodium lamps alternating

Exactly the world module's 'a light on water is a broken vertical streak'. Paint the streaks as a separate translucent card under the town lamps so they can be pulled out when the sun rises (harbor_lights_out).

set

wet stone quay with lamp pools, low dark hill ridge across the water, a scatter of town lights at the waterline, starred navy sky

The whole layer order of the harbour set in one frame: sky / far ridge with lights / water / quay stone. The stone's wet slabs are the floor card for the footprint trail of M3.

texture

a puddle mirroring red columns and blue neon signs down a long arcade

The club sidewalk floor: a glossy black floor card with painted red/blue reflections reads as rain and nightlife without animating any rain. Only used in the opening 09.1a-b.

mood

rainy street with power lines, a lit corner shop, red and teal smears on wet asphalt

The film cut already opens on it; the shopfront glow and overhead wires are the back layer of the club street before he reaches the water.

prop

a gibbous moon with clear maria on black

Reference for the moon card: a cream disc with three small soft maria, not a lamp and not a face (the module rejected both).

blocking

worm's-eye view up a canyon of towers to a single bright moon

In the film cut. Gives the one upward insert for 'we look at the moon the way we used to see it': the poet's POV between buildings before we cut to the two backs on the quay.

light

a red glass door glowing at the end of a dark blue alley, its light spilling on wet pavement

How the phone booth should read: the only warm lit box on a cold street, light spilling out as a painted wedge on the pavement card.

blocking

a lone figure at a railing above a teal, fogged city; a single red reflection on the wet deck

The last shot (09.4d) and the quay-lip staging: one small dark figure against a huge toned field, rail as a single thin card.

set

a concrete opening framing bright night water, a bench in the dark foreground

Frame-within-frame for the mother's living room: her big window must show the harbour sidewalk where the breakup plays, brighter than the room.

blocking

a woman with a full afro seen from behind on a waterfront boardwalk, watching the water

MOTHER at the curb watching the breakup with the poet (09.2c): the woman puppet from behind, small against the water, before the cut to her face.

Insights

  • Build one harbour and relight it four times rather than four sets: the light is the clock (moon, dead hour, grey, sunrise).
  • The cord is the call. Keep it dashed and pulsing; it is the cheapest way to say 'on the phone' across a wide frame.
  • The breakup must keep playing in the background of every call: use a second, smaller POET + EX pair on a far layer, looped.
  • Dissolves are rationed: only the mother leaving, the father arriving and EX's haze. Every other change is a cut.
  • Aging must never be literal: years pass as the moon climbing.
  • Hold the blue accent back to the final seconds; it is the only time blue appears in the poem.

  • Harbour quay kit and the moon/sun discs return in 13 (the harbour) and the sunrise light feeds 11.

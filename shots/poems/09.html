POEM 09 · The Shrouded Arcade

Yet, Heard

Three calls made at the edge of a harbour before leaving: to the lover under a shared moon, to the mother over a black hole that opens in the water, to the father who walks out of the eastern sky. One night on one quay, and the light is the clock: moon high, the dead hour, first grey, the sun coming up out of the harbour mouth. Each call is a dashed line of light strung between two people, and behind every call the same breakup keeps playing on the sidewalk.

Night navy and harbour teal from the bay stills (3238a164 lamps on black water, ef9f6c7e night quay); sodium amber of the quay lamps and the town across the water from the same two; neon red only in the club sidewalk opening (b05d5b9a puddle, 7fdacba9 street); the moon is paper cream, never pure white. Dawn walks the sky from slate grey (#8a8f9a) to sunrise apricot (#f2b35a) in M3-M4. Electric blue (#2f6bff) is the poem's single accent: 'someone blue', one point of light on the poet's chest in the last few seconds, nowhere else.

Two districts, one night. The Shrouded Arcade (03:30, rain) is the club sidewalk and the booth curb: paint the facades flat and dark (#272225 / #394b5a), and let ALL colour come from coded light: pink-magenta neon (#be5791) and red-blue signs as glowing strips cut into the cards, doubled underneath as wobbling reflection strips on a glossy black floor card. The booth is the only white light, a cold rectangle that spills a trapezoid onto the curb. The LED canopy is a sky card of moving colour held high, never lower than the +4 m line; we see its underside only, never its ends. Mirror Thread Bay (the inner harbour, pre-dawn) is water first: frame low, reflections before subjects; the quay is a dark stone strip, the water a black glossy card with lamp-columns of teal and sodium orange broken into dashes. The light is the clock: neon and moon, then the dead hour, then the orange haze of first light (views/085, 080) where everything flattens to two values. Never show: daylight crowds, the canopy's structure, the EX's face, any colour in the mother's room that isn't lamp-warm.

Insights

  • Build one harbour and relight it four times rather than four sets: the light is the clock (moon, dead hour, grey, sunrise).
  • The cord is the call. Keep it dashed and pulsing; it is the cheapest way to say 'on the phone' across a wide frame.
  • The breakup must keep playing in the background of every call: use a second, smaller POET + EX pair on a far layer, looped.
  • Dissolves are rationed: only the mother leaving, the father arriving and EX's haze. Every other change is a cut.
  • Aging must never be literal: years pass as the moon climbing.
  • Hold the blue accent back to the final seconds; it is the only time blue appears in the poem.
  • Atlas: the Halfworld frames (foundry/09-02, 09-03) already solve the poem in cut-out terms: two flat silhouettes, one paper moon, a dashed arc between heads. Match their flatness; don't let the call become a glowing tube.
  • Atlas: in the Arcade all colour belongs to light (neon strips, reflections, the booth's white); paint the walls nearly black so the coded light reads.
  • Atlas: the 09-to-10 cut is physical: booth and bike share one lamp on one curb. Park the bike card in the dark of 09.5 so 10 starts without a cut.

  • Harbour quay kit and the moon/sun discs return in 13 (the harbour) and the sunrise light feeds 11.
  • Elder FATHER walk cycle can be reused for the remembered elders in 12.
  • The dot-dissolve card technique carries to the ghost child and to 10's dawn sweep.
  • The poet's suitcase leaves with him; the empty sidewalk where 10 starts is this sidewalk at the end of the night.

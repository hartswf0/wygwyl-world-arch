POEM 11 · Cenotaph Rail

New Day

The poet dismounts at a ruined chapel on a foggy shore and, as the morning comes, a new temple builds itself with him: white, clean, modern, counted into being piece by piece. He walks laps round its edgeless pool, reads from a journal, walks on the still sea, and in the last movement the sun clears the ridge and a line of light runs across the valley to the finished temple and to him.

Blue hour and slate from the night shore still (ef9f6c7e); fog grey from 993cc7a2; dark tree-green hills and soft sea blue-green from the pool stills (08fe12af, 58cbb4ca). The temple is chalk white, never pure white, warmed by dawn rose and sun gold. Rust (#a94022, the district colour) is kept for the chapel stone and rubble, the old that the new replaces. The world's single accent is the finial's tip, reached only when the building is complete.

From Cenotaph Rail to the White Temple: the Shore is where time-lapse is the native tense, so every change is a card arriving or a band swapping, never a camera move through space. Cenotaph Rail gives the arrival: wet stone shore steps under the mountain, Wardens' lanterns still burning at dawn, a cold grey sea; it has no light of its own. The White Temple has no lamps either: its light is the white ceramic lit by the sky, and its pools hold the sky exactly. Paint the temple chalk white with one cyan seam (the Glaziers' line, the brightest seam in the city) and keep the chapel's dark stone walls visible under the flood line inside it. Pools are mirror cards: the temple painted inverted, sky first. Hills are one dark ridge silhouette reused in every movement; fog is a flat grey band card. Never show: ornament, gold, crowds (12 has the hall full), a sun drawn below the ridge.

Hero buildings

The Shore Steps

the bike comes down to the shore at the foot of the steps (11.0); the lanterns on the landings are still burning from the Wardens' dusk lighting.

Build as cards: a stair as a stack of 6-8 stepped strips with a landing every storey, wet-sheen layer on each tread; lantern cards with a small coded flame on each landing; the old station wall as a tall dark card at left; water card at the bottom edge. Lanterns go out one by one as the sun clears the ridge.

The Temple's Pools

the infinity pool he walks round and reads at (11.4) and the still water that doubles the finished temple in the welcome (11.5); laid where the chapel's floor was.

Build as cards: each pool is a glossy mirror card with the temple, columns and sky painted inverted and slightly darker; an edge-less lip (no rim card on the sea side) so the pool meets the sea line; ripple is a coded displacement only when he steps; the tambourine shards set into the lip as tiny bright plates.

STYLE (panel C): flat cards cut from corrugated cardboard, gouache with confident ink linework, the flutes faintly showing through the paint, a thin raw kraft-brown cut edge round every card, brass split-pin brads; shot flat-on, even light, no shadows, no perspective. Copy the craft from C, not the clothes in it. WHAT (panel B): the temple site: the same shore where the rubble lay, the white temple assembling on it, hills behind, sea in front Light: dawn from the horizon up (M2), rays cut out of the air (M3), terminator sweeping west across the valley (M6). SHEET: the place as five separate flat cardboard layers, one per band, painted to stack front to back in a multiplane rig: SKY: night bands lifting to dawn; BACK: same hill ridge, valley floor in shadow; MID: temple pieces on one ground line; FRONT: poet, rubble; FLOOR: rubble under the platform. Each layer is its own card with a clean cut top edge and transparent gaps where the layer behind shows through; keep the horizon on the blue line and the floor on the green line in every band so they register. Insights

  • There is only ever one drawing of the temple: the same 19 cards on the same ground line in every movement, just more of them.
  • Every change of light goes one way and is a hard swap (fog edge, dawn bands, ray wedges, terminator), never a dimmer.
  • Pieces arrive and then do not move; the stillness between arrivals is what makes them feel built.
  • The pool is the one place a mirror is honest: shoot the top half and flip it, no visible edge.
  • Walking on water is a calm patch that travels with him, not ripples reacting to him.
  • Keep the poet darker than the white stone and clear of the platform footprint, or the building swallows him.
  • Atlas: the Halfworld/markov frames (foundry/11-03, 11-04, sets/5380dc5ec9f0f55c-5) already build the temple as a black six-column silhouette counted up out of a dotted ground; our white 19 cards should read identically in silhouette at blue hour.
  • Atlas: the temple has no lamps; its light is white ceramic lit by the sky and doubled in the pools. Paint the pools as mirror cards and let the sky carry every change.
  • Atlas: 11 and 12 share one frame (the mirror hall / portico pool). Lock that plate's layout now so 12 is the same cards, filled.

For later poems

  • The completed white temple can stand in 14 as the sacred place everyone gathers in.
  • The hill ridge and sea carry into 13's harbour light and 14.
  • The journal returns whenever the poet reads aloud.
  • The 19-piece assembly technique is the template for any later self-building architecture.

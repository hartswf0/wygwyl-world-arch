POEM 08 · Ankhu Spires

Newly Single

He leaves the party for an open-air rooftop club over Atlantis, all strobes and blue-pink light, where the dancers smear in shifted time and he stands still and empty. His soul, a glowing see-through copy of him, peels out and floats up, watches the whole city lit below, is set down on a white-hot plain at the equator of a giant planet, and is cooled by angels pouring ice baths; back in the club the same water lands on his body under the strobe. Haze fills the room, the soul comes home, he leans back and breathes it in, and ends on the ledge staring at far mountains, then walks out into a street where he glimpses the EX's back and cannot reach him.

Night #0d1630 and spire blue #3a6fd8 from the Ankhu sheet (cbb5e9c5 aerial, 646ab0f0 misty platforms, 993cc7a2 deck). Purple #9b5fd0 and pink #ff5fa8 are the disc-spires of 959cd1f5 and the treatment's 'blue and pink lights throughout the city'. Soul blue-white #bfe6f2 is the ice blue of the aerial 434b7bdd and the soul glow. Red plain #b0303a and white sun #fbf6ea from 7a904269 (the red ground under a white light). District orange #d36f46 for the one accent: the spark at the soul's head (world module: never on the flesh).

Ankhu Spires is Atlantis from above: mushroom-stem towers with flat platform crowns joined by bridges, gardens and laundry on the platforms, cloud below and clear sky above. In cardboard that is a vertical stack: a cloud bank card cutting every stem at the same height, platform crowns as flat discs on thin stems (dowel or rolled card), violet and blue underlight on the stems (coded gradient on the stem cards, #3f5375/#1c3148), warm lamp points on the crowns. The club is an open crown two platforms over from a laundry roof; bridges are thin cards with rail ticks. The salt line rides up the stems at 4/10 of their height, far above any flood: paint it as a pale band on every stem. Never show the ground in the club shots; the only ground the poem gets is the soul's red-white equator plain and, at the very end, the wet street. The red of the shaft is the only red in Ankhu; keep the club violet/blue/pink.

Insights

  • Never merge the two copies: even after the return keep both cards in frame, the soul printed on acetate over the body. The line never says they became one thing again.
  • Time shift is a frame-rate contrast: dancers on 2s and 3s with ghost doubles, the poet on 1s. Cheap in cut-out, and it reads instantly.
  • 'Much hotter' is a subtraction: blow the sky away until the plain stands in white; do not add flames.
  • Intercut body and soul on the pour: the same water lands on both, which is how the poem's 'cool me down' connects two places.
  • The planet is a floor: a horizon so wide it barely curves; one red-white ground card and ticks, nothing scattered on it (open means empty).
  • Strobes: keep flashes under safe rates; let the haze and the colour carry the club instead.
  • The last stanza and the street beat bleed into 09 (Yet Heard): the EX's back through the crowd is the hinge.
  • Atlas: Ankhu's architecture is stems and crowns over a cloud bank; one cloud card that cuts every stem at the same height makes the rooftop club read as high without ever showing the ground.
  • Atlas: foundry/08-03 shows the soul as a stippled silhouette over the floor tiles; perforate the soul cards (pin-punched acetate) rather than only tinting them, so light passes through and the copy reads as hollow.
  • Atlas: the equator plain can reuse the red soil of the Night Field (views/071) pushed to white-hot light: the soul goes back to the ancestors' ground without a word.

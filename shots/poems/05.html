POEM 05 · The Ascent

Bloodlines

Still night: the poet walks out of the Nevermore field into a parade of afro-futurist ancestors who put stars in his hands, and he names them one by one until they are a constellation of his kin. The same nine-point constellation comes back as the Southern-pine joists over the basement mattress he grew up on, as the veins of blood-light in his fingerprint, and as the veining in the marble wings of a gate that finally opens. It is a poem about inheritance getting bigger: a small man under a wide sky becomes a print too big for the frame, then a gate that runs off all four edges.

Night blue #0a1230 and the district colour #2560e1 come from the Ascent sheet (2ce9173b blue city under stars, 5dcae595 milky way over spires). Indigo #2a2f7a is the ancestors' robes. Star white-gold #f4e3b0 is the laundry-rooftop star cluster (e9c68c53) and the nebula in 36c9c157. The single accent #ffc94a is MAE, the heir-light, the one star he is given rather than takes (world module: one accent on one node in every costume; none in M3). Ember #e08a3c is the bonfire under the ribbed arch in 36c9c157 (parade lantern glow). Southern pine #b9874f and the basement's dark #3a2a22 are from the cramped wood-and-concrete stairwell 34a1d5c3. Marble #e8e4dc for the winged gate.

The Night Field & the Ascent is the only district in Atlantis without lamps: every warm light on screen must be something a hand is carrying (star orb, lantern staff, galaxy disc) or the moon. Paint the field as two cards only: a deep night-blue sky (#072353 into #194c97) pinholed and back-lit for stars, and a red-brown soil rise (#6f4e48/#332123) curving as a convex horizon as in views/071. Architecture is almost nothing: the avenue's stones laid with the emblem, one obelisk silhouette, one lone tree, the gate's ring as a thin pale arc on the horizon. Code only the moving lights (orbs, tie-lines, type-on names, the hymn rings); everything else is a static painted plate. Never show a street lamp, a window light, or a neon sign in the field; the first manufactured light the audience sees is the cyan fence of the Perimeter at the very end, and that contrast is the cut into 06. The basement memory belongs to The Black: no geography around it, just the pine joists and the concrete in one warm bulb pool, framed by black.

Insights

  • One graph, four costumes: lay out the nine-node constellation once (KIN coordinates in the world module) and reuse it as sky, joists, fingerprint veins and marble veining. The eye recognises it before it can say why; that is the poem's continuity.
  • Scale is the arc: lock the camera and grow the subject (small man, wide sky in M1 -> print larger than frame in M4 -> gate past all four edges in M5). Cut-out makes this cheap: same cards, bigger prints.
  • Spend the single accent (#ffc94a) only on Mae's star, wherever it is; M3 gets none because he owns nothing yet.
  • The basement must be uncomfortable without being sad-coloured: keep it in the same blue night with one warm bulb, and let the window well show the named stars so the past is still under his sky.
  • The gates stanza is spoken at the head of the 06 reading (06 recording 00:00.0), while 05's film and world module stage it as 05's final movement; cut the gate opening to that line so the two poems hand off across it.
  • Avoid a spray of stars: named stars are holes of light, unnamed ones stay tiny specks; a ring of evenly spaced stars reads as diagram, not family.
  • Ancestors' hands on his are the most intimate image in the suite so far; give the hand cards real weight and slow timing.
  • Atlas: the Ascent is the only lamp-less district: keep every warm light in a hand. That makes the cyan Perimeter fence the shock that sells the cut into 06.
  • Atlas: views/068's rib vault gives the Parade Ground a ceiling; three depths of pierced brown card over a nebula plate turn the open field into a procession hall without adding architecture.
  • Atlas: foundry/05-02 and 05-03 already draw the name plates and the ring-and-node web as flat black-on-cream graphics; print them directly as cut-out cards rather than re-designing.

POEM 02 · The Overseer

Flashing Lights

A silent scream: a scream whose rings travel inward. The man floats in black while walls cave on him, wakes after midnight to a phone that rings twice and a mirror that answers his hand exactly, then his pupil opens and we go through it into a winter living room where he is six, a dead seven-year-old stands one pencil notch taller, his parents argue in a car with its hazards flashing, and he throws the tambourine she gave him through the glass.

The district is The Overseer: deep indigo night and electric blue from 18a6390f (blue skyline over black water, red beacon streaks) and ec036b41 (moon in a ring of towers). The flashing lights are three colours only: headlight white (#e8edf5), hazard amber (#f0a830) and the red beacon of 3603f621 (#d8322e). #7f97b8 is the ghost child's grey-blue wash. #5aa7ff is the suite accent, given here to the phone glow and the far light at the end of the road.

Two districts. The Black (02.0-02.1) again: no geography, no flood line, no seam; a black stage where the only light is strobing practicals and the figure lit alone, so build nothing behind him but black velvet card and let flash gels do the work. The Overseer (02.2-02.5) is watched, cold and symmetrical: after midnight in winter, one-point perspective with the moon dead on the axis between two slab towers, as if the moon were a lamp hung over the street. Paint the whole outside in five blues (#091a38 to #3d6a9a, accent #1078da) with no street lamps on the towers' side: moonlight, screen-blue, one crimson beacon high on a tower and the car's headlights and amber hazards are the only warm things. Towers are flat slab cards with blue pipework and tiny lit windows punched and backed with blue gel; the roofless palace sits on the ridge as a silhouette. The house is a brick rowhouse with a marble stoop and a big street-facing ground-floor window. The hill is under no line: do not draw a salt line or ceramic plates here; a clean wall is honest in this district. Inside the room keep it dark with one warm lamp so the window is a cold blue screen the child stands against in silhouette.

Insights

  • Everything in this world collapses inward: rings, dark, sound. Animate every radiating thing backwards and the poem's grammar is done for you.
  • Size the two children to the door post, not to each other: one notch is the difference between the living and the dead.
  • Rooms here are filled dark fields with holes cut in them; the holes (mirror, bed, window) are the only lit paper. Outlined rooms on cream read as diagrams.
  • The mirror is free: build POET's half and flip it on the centre line so the hands meet exactly.
  • Three light colours only (white, amber, red) and never mixed in one sweep, or flashing becomes disco.
  • The tambourine must leave whole: its breaking belongs to 03.
  • Atlas: the Overseer is symmetry: put the moon exactly on the axis between two towers and the child exactly centred in the window. Any off-centre composition loses the sense of being watched.
  • Atlas: no salt line and no ceramic plates on the hill; a clean wall is honest here. Do not carry the 01/03 dressing into this street.
  • Atlas: sets/5e0fe00a4402b2bb-1 (boy from behind, blue window, sedan at the curb) is the master shot of the poem; build the living-room card stack to reproduce it.

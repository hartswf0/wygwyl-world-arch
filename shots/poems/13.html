POEM 13 · Mirror Thread Bay

How to Win My Heart

The poet walks down from the white temple to a corner café on the harbor of the heavens and sits at a small table, watching couples, while the tide comes in under the boats. Dusk counts out Jupiter's moons over the water; his younger self watches from the rail, the elders pray with their pearls, and his love letters burn on the quay stones. He harvests a field of scarlet poppies and purple blossoms until it flowers out of his own arms, and then, across the water, a distant window comes into focus with a woman in it, and he takes off his sunglasses and says hello.

Bay blue #465fb1 is the poem's colour in film/poems.json and the dusk sky over the harbor; harbor teal #0f5f63 and night navy #10243f are the water under the boardwalk and the night pier (932cb5b5_2, 3238a164_1, fc83bfee_0). Lantern gold #f0a64a is the café's string lights and paper lanterns (22ddf809_2, 932cb5b5_2). Quay grey #5c6670 is wet stone. The two flower colours are the accents: scarlet poppy #c8231e and purple blossom #7a3fa0, and SHE's red dress takes the poppy scarlet so the field and the window rhyme. Linen cream #e9dcc4 is the couples' summer clothes and the letters.

Mirror Thread Bay is water first: reflection before subject. Every set card that touches the water gets a doubled, rippled copy beneath it, and we often frame the reflection and let the person arrive into it. Daytime here is turquoise and calm (13.0-13.5), then the haze rises off the water (13.6). Paint the quay as old harbour stone with the flood line at +4 m everywhere, tide boards at every pier (white boards with black height marks), benches facing the water. The café is teal paint with whitewash trim, plank deck, slate roof, striped awnings, string lights, ships' masts behind. Light technology is daylight on water: one big coded caustic shimmer on the underside of awnings and the café ceiling. Code the shimmer; paint the stone. The one other light is the haze, an orange-to-white wash that rises from the bottom of frame at the end. Never show neon or the spire city in close here; the Atlantis skyline is a far flat silhouette band across the water only.

Insights

  • One quantity per movement and it only goes one way: tide in, dark down, smoke up, field down, focus in. Pick the object that shows each number and never let it turn round.
  • Build the harbor as one tonal pass (sky, mud, water, quay) so day, dusk and night are the same set relit, and the tide is a moving line, not a redrawn object.
  • SHE is tiny and far: give her a dedicated small-scale head and keep her eyes to seven points; at full puppet scale she will read as a doll.
  • The becoming must stay HIS body with flowers growing out of it; a torso-and-stems substitute reads as a scarecrow.
  • The red of her dress is the poppy red: the field he harvests and the woman he hopes for are the same colour.
  • Transcript corrections from the treatment: 'Ice has frozen in thought' is 'Ice has frozen and thawed friendships'; 'at time and tears have just claimed' is 'that time and tears have disclaimed'; 'windless seasons' is 'winless seasons'.
  • Atlas: the halfworld frames (foundry/13-02, 13-03) already solve the poem in flat halftone cut-out: boats, chapel crosses, a gable window, couples as single silhouettes. Use them as the style key for every distance layer.
  • Atlas: the pier café hero (striped awning, plank deck, masts, bulb string) is a single reusable build; plan it so the window wall can carry a reflection layer for the YOUNG_POET beat and the 13.5 reverse.
  • Atlas: frame the reflection first and let the subject arrive into it; the bay's whole camera language is low over water.
  • Atlas: the haze rise at 13.6 is the same build as 14.0; build it once in 13.

For later poems

  • The harbor, the temple on its rise and the boats are what the haze takes first in 14.
  • The poppy field is the 'field' card in 14's reel.
  • The couples-in-orbit move becomes 14's two entwined muses (the same turn, both him).
  • The window motif closes here as a window that looks back; in 14 the window stays shut and he chooses a door.

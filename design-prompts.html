A city is a set of relationships that survives the next cut.

The street and the room must agree. The person who receives an object must still own it after a camera change. A closed door must affect the route and the sound. These briefs turn those relationships into buildable constraints.

Each prompt can be copied in full. Its source links name existing material; the operations and acceptance scenes describe the work to build. Keep recorded evidence, measured checks, inferred structure and invented additions visible in the resulting records.

Evolve the interior

Search the space inside a building while its exterior, windows and stairs remain accountable.

The current set data counts room imagery as buildings when generating exterior density. The hero descriptions already provide rooms, doors, windows and stairs. The next step is to connect those two kinds of evidence.

Extend the Foundry from landscapes to inhabited volumes. Begin with the Severn entry in the existing hero descriptions and its interior reference photographs. Select poem-linked references from the Foundry sets and Archive. Preserve the declared 20 by 12 metre envelope, storey heights, stair landings, facade openings, building identity and source IDs. Classify each reference as exterior, threshold, interior, object or body. Give a room reference its own spatial evidence record.

Represent a candidate as rooms connected by doors and stairs, with window bearings, camera positions, prop footprints and named human actions. Mutate partitions, interior door positions, furniture arrangements, practical lights and occupancy. Record the seed, parent and mutation. Reject a candidate before ranking when it leaves the envelope, overlaps another room, obstructs the stairs, detaches a window from its facade, or prevents an actor from reaching a required beat.

Reuse the existing expand, prove and build contracts. Extend their prototype checks with actor clearance, door swing, camera clearance and window-to-street consistency. Rank surviving candidates against several views: hallway depth, door rhythm, foreground occlusion, measured light regions and the required action sequence. Preserve a diverse set of plausible plans. Treat concealed space as alternative hypotheses with explicit uncertainty; a photograph does not uniquely specify a floor plan. A completed 360-degree panorama supplies a hypothesis for the exterior or window view, not recovered interior topology.

Evaluate occupancy as carefully as geometry. A chair needs room for a person to sit and stand. A table needs an approach. A window needs a view consistent with its position outside. Attach sound transmission and practical light to the same door and opening records that control circulation. Keep the building's authored history and material evidence attached to every candidate.

Deliver a plan, section, room graph, provenance record, aligned reference comparison and reversible candidate selection. Acceptance scene: approach from the street, enter the lobby, climb to the second floor, pass the numbered doors, enter flat 2A and look through its actual facade window. Close the door; the route and sound transmission must both change. Save and reopen at the same camera, hour and state.

Stage an inhabited shot

Give the landscape an encounter, a foreground, a destination and a consequence.

Select one strong Foundry shot with its exact world, poem, start and end time, camera, lens and source image. Use WEAVE and the existing multiplane stack as the visual floor. Write the shot treatment before adding objects: who is doing what, to whom, with which object, through which opening, and what changes before the cut. Choose a social action with a visible result: an arrival, a delivery, a shared seat, a gate being opened or a repair being completed.

Retrieve compatible people, vehicles, furniture, trees and facade fragments from the Archive and existing figure kits. Preserve provenance and distinguish recorded, generated and staged material. Assemble foreground contact, human action, middle-distance architecture and distant sky as separate depth layers. Give each cutout a scale anchor, ground contact, pivot, alpha edge, depth range and permitted viewing cone. Add geometry for floor contact, occlusion, doorway passage, shadows and the intended camera move.

Use several views or a rig when a person turns. Restrict the camera where the source support ends and mark the unsupported view in the authoring tools. Place trees where soil, roots and passage allow them. Place a vehicle with a reason to stop, load, wait or leave. Show tire contact, a usable curb and a destination. Let a secondary figure respond to the primary action instead of filling the background with unrelated motion.

Bind light to an identifiable window, lamp or sky opening. Preserve a quiet region around the action. Let weather change specific surfaces and clothing rather than every pixel. Link each sound to an action or source position; keep the source recording dominant while it speaks. Build time into the encounter through anticipation, contact, release and the trace left behind.

Deliver three aligned images: original reference, dressed still and frame from the traversable set, plus a short animatic and layer manifest. Acceptance scene: hold the camera as a resident arrives, hands an object to another and crosses a threshold. Show foreground occlusion and release, grounded feet, light through the doorway and the audible change when it closes. Repeat from the reverse angle with the same building, identities and prop ownership.

Inspect the hero buildings

Make aura emerge from a building's openings, materials, use and surrounding light.

The hero compiler already describes six buildings and runs prototype checks. Those checks provide a starting point for visual and spatial inspection; they do not constitute construction certification.

Inspect all six HDL heroes: Severn, chapel, NEW SINGLE, pier café, clock tower and Atlantis rowhouse. Compare each with its existing exterior and interior reference walls. Keep the plan, door graph, material names, facts register and poem beats linked to one asset ID. Start with a neutral daylight pass. Repair scale, normals, wall thickness, gaps, texture repetition, floor joins, stairs and window depth before grading the image.

Preserve the distinction between reference evidence, checked geometry, inferred structure and invented additions. Build an aura sheet from observable relationships: where light enters, which surface receives it, which recess stays dark, what has worn through use, what is audible behind the wall and what changes during occupation. Record those relationships as reproducible lighting and material settings with source notes.

Use a small, coherent material family in each place. Give textures a metre-based scale and brick, painted wood, glass, damp stone and metal distinct roughness. Attach the Severn's red and teal color to identifiable fixtures and exterior sources. Restrict wet reflection to wet surfaces. Preserve the unlit material color and readable faces. Let a warm doorway be warm because of its interior lamps, and let that light fall across the pavement and a passing body.

Produce the same eye-level exterior, threshold, occupied interior and view back out in daylight and at the authored poem hour. Compare identical lens, camera and exposure. Retain a reference overlay and a raw lighting toggle. Check window mullions, floor edges and stair treads at close range as well as the building's distant silhouette. A dramatic wide shot cannot substitute for a usable threshold.

Deliver one corrected hero description and one reusable lighting/material recipe consumed by both the hero viewer and city. Acceptance scene: a person approaches, enters, climbs, occupies a room and exits. The room reads at seated and standing eye height. Its exterior window opens onto that same room. Silhouette and material hierarchy remain recognizable with bloom, grain and colored fill disabled. Restore the theatrical grade after the building passes.

Compile the worldtext

Let a reading change the rules of access, encounter, memory and movement.

Build from the existing Gazetteer, architectural language, district scene book and poem production briefs. Begin with the Severn bedroom and fire escape, the House and back stair, and the chapel becoming temple and reunion. Treat each account as a situated description with a speaker, date, source passage and point of view. Convert its consequences into operations: who can enter, what is exchanged, what can be heard through a wall, which path becomes available, and which trace remains after a visit. Preserve alternate accounts when they disagree.

Use Calvino's method of interlacing city series as a compositional prompt. Build several readings of a shared place around memory, exchange, desire, signs and loss. Give each reading an observable spatial rule. A city of memory can retain worn paths after a building changes. A city of exchange can change its gates with the market schedule. A city of signs can give the same doorway different meanings to visitors and residents. These are proposed operations inspired by the book, not claims that the literary descriptions determine a unique simulation.

Connect the existing architectural rules to the building and scene records. The physical +4 metre flood datum changes material wear and access; keep it distinct from a small object’s symbolic stripe. Ceramic repair changes a visible join. Historical additions alter circulation. An archive skin changes what a surface records. Keep the rule, the evidence that motivated it, and the object it changes together. When a rule cannot yet be executed, label it as an unresolved requirement and preserve its source.

Let the author compare readings in the same camera and world state. Show the relevant source passage beside the changed doorway, route or encounter in the design tools. Keep explanatory prose outside the performance image unless it belongs inside the scene. Offer a dependable return to the original reading or recording. Preserve voice, cadence and ambiguity when a spatial interpretation selects only one of several possible meanings.

Deliver a source ledger, executable rule records, a district comparison and one scene with three alternate readings. Acceptance scene: two people approach the same gate with different histories. Their available actions differ for a traceable reason. Change the interpretation, replay the scene and inspect exactly what changed. The story remains connected to its words, bodies and place.

Read Atlantis through time

Make the map reveal the relation between a functioning city and the city read from its remains.

Start with the existing Nolli-like voice plan. Preserve its mappings: word duration becomes frontage, pauses become alleys, breaths become piazzas and line breaks become cross streets. The page uses Plato’s ring schema while deriving ring widths from poem durations. Keep these compositional rules distinct from metric building dimensions. Register the plan, architectural section and human-height camera with an explicit scale transform. Preserve poem, line interval, block ID and source audio in each view.

Use Verne’s underwater Atlantis chapter, “A Vanished Continent,” as a second reading of the place. Its night walk, obstructed approach, red volcanic light and revealed ruins offer a sequence of discovery. Stage arrival before overview. Preserve the distinction between that literary account, the existing poetic Atlantis and invented connections. Record each landmark’s living use, submerged trace, source and alignment uncertainty. Keep the later submarine shelter in an extinct volcano a separate episode.

Design a journey that changes viewpoint through evidence. Begin on a familiar street with people using it. Enter a building, look back through its window and recognize the same bridge. Cross into a submerged reading through an authored transition in time or account. Preserve enough geometry that the stair, opening and former street can still be identified. Change water level, visibility, material condition and occupation together. Reveal scale through the body and the duration of the crossing.

Connect the map to the SCION screening workflow through a shot itinerary. Each stop needs a map position, source passage, before/after keyframe, camera move, human action and transition condition. Use cutouts and existing scene layers where they support the composition. Reserve full geometry for necessary traversal and occlusion. Keep the map readable while the image becomes cinematic; a visitor must be able to return from the shot to its place.

Deliver an aligned plan/section, a living-to-submerged landmark register and a six-shot route with production prompts. Acceptance scene: follow one threshold from inhabited room to underwater ruin, then return to the map. The same opening, stair direction and bridge relationship survive. The account and time layer remain explicit, so a compelling transition never silently becomes a factual reconstruction.

Give the image depth

Keep the photographic composition while making supported movements through it believable.

Choose a shot from the existing Shot Lab and preserve its source image, masks, estimated depth, camera and layered redraw. Use WEAVE's contact and registration quality as the baseline. Define the intended motion before reconstructing the set: a small lateral move, a walk through a doorway, a turn toward a window, or a reverse angle. State which source views support that motion and which areas require new evidence or invented completion.

Separate image planes, estimated geometry, collision surfaces and navigable ground. Keep their coordinate transforms explicit. Establish a trustworthy scale anchor and camera horizon. Use the existing ground convention and inspect the result against a standing person, a step and a door. Repair stretched depth edges, floating fragments, gaps, bad normals and contradictory surfaces. Preserve the original composition as a saved camera that can be restored exactly.

Build a hybrid set appropriate to each region. Keep a distant facade as a plane when the camera will not expose its edge. Model a doorway, handrail, tread or table where bodies touch it or pass it. Use multiple views for a turning character. Give foliage depth and contact at the level required by the shot. Mark unsourced surfaces and provide alternate completions when the evidence is ambiguous. Keep the authored viewing range visible in the editor.

Inspect a neutral pass before atmosphere. Give paving, soil, masonry and water different material behavior; match texture scale and contact shadows to the same world units. Add district light and weather only after the ground and openings work. Use distance-aware detail so the strongest close view remains stable while distant context becomes cheaper. Compare the original, the layered composition and the spatial view side by side at the source camera.

Deliver the registered scene, camera limits, confidence/provenance masks and a short comparison film. Acceptance scene: begin at the source photograph, move sideways far enough to expose a foreground overlap, walk to the threshold and look inside. Feet stay grounded, objects retain scale, occlusion changes correctly and the source camera can be recovered. Stop or request evidence where the reconstruction ceases to support the move.

Hear a place changing

Use replayable timing and a restrained mix of sounds with visible or discoverable causes.

Existing tools already contain recordings and spatial sound. The work here is to make their timing, sources and mix coherent. A pre-rendered station mix needs a new audio render when its echo is baked into the file.

Audit Radio Atlantis, Poemworlds, the duets, the Observatory and the movie map's sound system. Identify which audio is an original recording, a rendered mix or a live effect. Preserve the original speaker, pitch and timing; keep a dry reference available. Separate voice, close action, local ambience, distant city and music into controllable buses. Begin the mix with the voice and one environmental source, then add a layer only when it explains an action, distance, material or change of time.

Give every sound a source object or bounded region, an activation rule, a gain range and a relation to the shared world clock. A passing vehicle follows its route. Footsteps follow contacts. Room tone changes at a threshold. A lamp hum belongs to the lamp. Doors affect transmission and reverberation using the same state that affects navigation. Keep a quiet listening mode and an obvious mute control. Start audio only after an explicit user action.

Remove excessive voice doubling and feedback from the source mix or effect graph that creates them. Default delay sends to zero. Add a reflection only for a specific room, surface or authored transition. Place sung replies after lines, following the duets’ call-and-response structure. Keep the spoken recording intelligible before spatial treatment. Limit simultaneous nearby events, budget the distant bed and duck it during speech. Use silence as a timed state. Let time be visible as well: a delivery ends, a room empties, a lamp switches on, and a patch of light moves across a surface.

For Freesound material, save the sound ID, creator, source URL, exact license and attribution alongside the downloaded asset. Check the individual sound's terms; the API's terms are a separate matter. Keep replaceable source slots so a licensed recording can be exchanged without rewriting the scene. Record trim points, fades, loop boundaries and loudness adjustments.

Deliver the event score, source ledger, bus controls and a reproducible clock/seed. Acceptance scene: hear a street from outside a room, enter, close the door, listen to a line of the poem and reopen it. The acoustic change follows the opening. Compare the same passage on headphones and a phone speaker. Pause, seek and replay from the same state; action, voice, light and sound return together, without a burst of accumulated events.

Keep one world across tools

Make the city map, interior solver, shot composer and player agree on the same place and state.

Choose one complete pilot location: the Severn, its adjacent street, a doorway, a room and a shot that uses them. Inspect the existing records consumed by the Foundry, hero viewer, city, island, movie map and shot tools. Record their units, axes, IDs, clock assumptions and asset references. Write adapters around those real contracts. Preserve render meshes, collision geometry and the room/door graph as separate outputs of the same building description. When the city renderer bakes hero meshes by material, retain the structures needed for navigation and sound transmission. Preserve source data and existing URLs while establishing one versioned scene record for the pilot.

The shared record must name the world, district, building, room, poem, source account and shot. It must carry the seed, world time, camera/lens, geometry transform, material/light recipe, cast states, prop ownership, door states, sound events and provenance. Use stable IDs to refer to assets. Keep representation-specific settings with their renderer. A cutout pivot and a mesh skeleton may differ while referring to the same person and action.

Make switching a view preserve the meaningful state. Move from the city map to an exterior shot, enter the interior, return to the multiplane composition and reopen the map. Keep the selected place, hour, people, object and completed events. Distinguish the performance clock from local hour, historical era and tide phase; author their mapping explicitly. Keep The Black’s deliberately nongeographic transitions outside the street network. A scrubbed film frame should reconstruct a valid scene state; resuming play should not fire old events twice.

Build the pilot at the scale a person experiences. Repair its ground, curb, tree placement, accessible route and neighboring massing. Use the best district references to establish a consistent material hierarchy. Carry existing street-life and sound capabilities into the pilot through the shared record. Compare the same eye-level camera across viewers; measure mismatches before expanding the city.

Deliver the scene schema, adapters, one complete location, save/reopen behavior and a short journey across representations. Acceptance scene: select the Severn on the map, approach with a parcel, enter and give it to a resident. Switch to the shot view. The resident still holds the parcel, the door and lighting retain their state, and the sound uses the same hour. Reload the saved record and repeat the journey. Expand to another district only when this whole encounter survives.
